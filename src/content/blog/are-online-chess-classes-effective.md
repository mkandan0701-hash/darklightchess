**Short answer:** yes, for most children. Live online chess classes work well because the coach can see every move on a shared board and correct mistakes straight away. They work best when the class is interactive, short enough for the child's age, and paired with practice and game review. Recorded videos on their own are much less effective than a live coach.

## Why chess suits online learning

Chess is already played on a board that translates perfectly to a screen. In a live online class, the coach and child share the same digital board, the coach can set up any position in seconds, and every game can be saved and reviewed later. Many children already play online, so the setting feels familiar.

## What makes an online class work

1. **It's live.** The coach watches your child think and asks questions, rather than playing a video.
2. **It has structure.** Each lesson builds on the last, following a curriculum.
3. **It's the right length.** Younger children do best with shorter, active sessions.
4. **There's practice between classes.** A few puzzles on most days makes a big difference.
5. **Games are reviewed.** Children improve fastest when a coach goes through their own games.

## Is it as good as learning in person?

For learning and improving at chess, a good live online class can match an in-person one. What in-person learning adds is the social side: playing over a real board and meeting other children. Many families get both by joining a local club or school chess team for in-person games, alongside online coaching for structured lessons.

Honest caveat: there is little rigorous research comparing online and in-person chess teaching for children. The points above come from coaching experience, not controlled studies.

## Does age matter?

- **Ages 4–6:** online works with short sessions and a parent nearby to help with the device. A physical board at home helps young children connect the screen to real pieces.
- **Ages 7–12:** most children are comfortable online and enjoy the interactive board.
- **Teenagers:** online is often the best format, since they can review games and study openings with the coach on screen.

## Signs online classes are working

- Your child can explain why they made a move.
- They blunder pieces less often.
- They use ideas from class in their own games.
- They want to play more.

## Frequently asked questions

### What does my child need for an online chess class?
A computer or tablet with a camera, a stable internet connection and a quiet space. A physical chess board is helpful but not required.

### How long should an online class be?
Younger children do well with shorter sessions; older children and tournament players often prefer an hour.

### Can my child play tournaments if they learn online?
Yes. Where your child competes doesn't depend on where the coach is. Our [tournament coaching](/tournament-chess-coaching/) prepares students for specific events.
