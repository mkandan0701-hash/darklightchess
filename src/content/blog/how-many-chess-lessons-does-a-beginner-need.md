**Short answer:** most complete beginners need about 20 to 25 weekly lessons, roughly three months, to play a full, legal game with basic tactics. Our Beginner level is 24 live classes for that reason. Children who practice for 15 minutes on a few days between lessons usually get there faster; children who only play in class take longer.

## What does "learning chess" mean for a beginner?

Parents often mean different things by "knows how to play". It helps to split it into three milestones:

1. **Knows the rules.** Every piece moves correctly, including castling, en passant and promotion, and the child recognizes check, checkmate and stalemate. Many children get here in 4 to 8 lessons.
2. **Plays a complete game.** The child can play from the first move to checkmate or a draw without help and without illegal moves. This usually takes 10 to 16 lessons.
3. **Plays a sensible game.** The child spots simple forks and pins, doesn't leave pieces hanging, follows basic opening principles and can checkmate with a queen or rook. This is the end of a beginner course: about 20 to 25 lessons.

## What changes the number of lessons?

- **Practice between lessons.** This matters most. A few puzzles and one short game on three or four days a week roughly doubles what sticks from each lesson. See [how often kids should practice chess](/blog/how-often-should-kids-practice-chess/).
- **Age.** Children aged 4 to 6 learn best in short, playful steps and may take longer to reach milestone 3. Older children and adults often move faster once the rules are clear. Our guide on [what age a child should start chess lessons](/blog/what-age-should-a-child-start-chess-lessons/) covers this.
- **Group or one-to-one.** A private lesson can move at exactly the child's pace, so a quick learner may need fewer lessons. A group gives more games against peers. Read [private vs group chess lessons](/blog/private-vs-group-chess-lessons/).
- **Consistency.** A fixed weekly slot beats bursts of lessons with long gaps.

## How do I know my child is ready to move on?

Look at what they do in games, not how many lessons they've had. A child is ready for the next level when they play a full game without illegal moves, deliver basic checkmates confidently, and apply opening principles without being reminded. A coach checks this in an assessment game.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can a child learn chess in one lesson?
They can learn how the pieces move in one or two lessons, but playing a real game takes several weeks of practice.

### Is one lesson a week enough?
For most beginners, yes, if they practice a little between lessons. Twice a week helps children who enjoy it and want to move faster.

### How long should a beginner chess lesson be?
About 30 to 45 minutes for children aged 4 to 7, and around an hour for older children and adults.

### Do adults need fewer lessons?
Often, yes. Adults usually learn the rules faster, though they still need practice games to build pattern recognition.
