**Short answer:** teach one piece at a time, starting with the pawns and rooks, and play a mini-game with each piece before adding the next. Keep sessions to 10 to 15 minutes for young children. Save checkmate for last, once every piece is familiar. You don't need to be a strong player: you're teaching rules and habits, not strategy.

## Do I need to be good at chess to teach my child?

No. To start, you only need to know how the pieces move, and you can learn that alongside your child. Being a beginner can even help: you'll explain things simply and enjoy discovering together. A coach becomes useful once your child knows the rules and wants to improve.

## In what order should I teach the pieces?

Teach them one or two at a time, with a mini-game for each:

1. **Pawns.** Play "pawn wars": each side has only eight pawns; the first to reach the other side wins.
2. **Rooks.** Add a rook each to pawn wars. Rooks are easy to understand because they move in straight lines.
3. **Bishops.** Show how they stay on one color. Play a game with pawns and bishops.
4. **Queen.** Explain that the queen moves like a rook and a bishop together.
5. **Knight.** Teach the "L" shape last among the pieces, since it's the hardest. A game of "knight hunts the pawns" helps.
6. **King.** Explain that the king can't be captured, only trapped. Now introduce check.
7. **Checkmate.** Teach it once all the pieces are familiar, starting with simple patterns.

Leave castling, en passant and promotion until the child plays full games.

## How do I keep a young child interested?

- **Keep it short.** Ten minutes of fun beats thirty minutes of struggle. Stop while they still want more.
- **Play mini-games, not full games.** Small goals give quick wins.
- **Tell stories.** Young children remember "the rook only drives on straight roads" better than a rule.
- **Let them win honestly.** Give yourself fewer pieces instead of making deliberate mistakes; children notice.
- **Praise thinking, not winning.** "You saw that your bishop was in danger" builds better habits than "well done for winning."

## What are common mistakes parents make?

- Teaching every rule in the first session.
- Correcting every move during a game. Pick one thing to point out afterwards.
- Always winning, or always letting the child win.
- Pushing full games before the child enjoys the mini-games.

## When should my child get a chess coach?

Consider a coach when your child knows the rules and asks to play often, wants to beat you or older siblings, or is curious about tournaments. A coach adds structure: a curriculum, tactics training and review of their own games. ## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best age to teach a child chess?
Many children can start around 4 to 6 with short, playful sessions. See [what age a child should start chess lessons](/blog/what-age-should-a-child-start-chess-lessons/).

### Should I use a chess app or a real board?
Start with a real board when you can. Moving physical pieces helps young children learn the rules, and playing together keeps it social.

### How often should we play?
A few short sessions a week is enough at the start.
