**Short answer:** the English Chess Federation (ECF) gives every player a rating based on their results in ECF-rated over-the-board events. A child gets one by playing in those events. You cannot buy one, and lessons alone do not create one. This guide uses the ECF's own rating pages, and you should check the [ECF rating FAQs](https://www.englishchess.org.uk/rating-faqs/) for the latest rules.

## What is an ECF rating?

It is a number that estimates a player's strength. Rating rises when a player does better than expected and falls when they do worse. The ECF runs its own system alongside the international FIDE rating, and rates many games FIDE does not, including games against new players.

## How does a child get a first rating?

By playing rated games. According to the ECF:

- A minimum of five games is needed for a published rating.
- Ratings based on fewer than ten games are labelled "Partial" and carry less weight.
- No one starts with a rating from nothing: the system needs at least one real result before it can produce one.

So the practical route is to enter junior and club events that are ECF-rated, and check the event's entry page, which says whether it is rated.

## What rating does a new junior start from?

The ECF sets a default starting point for a player's first calculation. For juniors under 18 it is 100 times their age, and for adults 1,800. The ECF reduces this by 100 for time controls other than standard play. The ECF updated its defaults from 1 September 2024, so check the rating pages for the current figures.

This default is only a starting point. A child's rating quickly moves to reflect real results.

## Why do junior ratings move faster?

Children generally improve quickly, so the ECF uses a higher K-factor for juniors when their rating is rising: 40 when rising and 20 when falling, according to the ECF guidance. That lets a fast-improving child's rating catch up with their real strength sooner.

## How often does a rating update?

Monthly. The ECF describes its system as a monthly rating list, so results from an event appear in a later list rather than straight away.

## Does my child need ECF membership?

Not to be rated. The ECF's FAQs explain that it rates non-members' games, because leaving them out would distort members' ratings. Membership matters for entering some events. According to the [ECF membership page](https://www.englishchess.org.uk/ecf-membership-rates-and-joining-details/):

- Juniors who have never been ECF members can get a free first year at Bronze or Gold level, but the sign-up must be completed.
- Bronze is generally enough for club, league and county play.
- Gold is needed for larger events and congresses.

Rules and prices change, so check the ECF page before you pay.

## Do online lessons help with an ECF rating?

Lessons do not give a rating. They prepare your child to play well in the events that do. Practical ways coaching helps:

- **Learning the rules of tournament play**, including touch-move and notation.
- **Reviewing games**, so a child learns from losses.
- **Preparing for a specific event** your child has entered.

## What if my child is in Scotland, Wales or Northern Ireland?

Each nation has its own chess federation, and the ECF covers England. The ideas are similar, but check your own federation for how ratings work.

## A simple plan for your child's first rating

1. Learn the basics and tournament etiquette in lessons.
2. Choose an ECF-rated junior or club event suited to beginners.
3. Complete any ECF membership or entry steps the organiser asks for.
4. Play at least five rated games. A first published rating comes after that.
5. Review the games with a coach, and aim for your next event.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the lowest age for an ECF rating?
The ECF's default rating is based on age, with no minimum age in the pages we read. Check an event's entry rules for age bands.

### Can a rating go down?
Yes. Losing to lower-rated players lowers it, and juniors' falling K-factor is smaller, so it falls more slowly than it rises.

### Is an ECF rating the same as a FIDE rating?
No. They are separate systems. The ECF also rates games FIDE excludes.

### Where can I find my child's rating?
On the ECF's rating site, which lists players and their results.
