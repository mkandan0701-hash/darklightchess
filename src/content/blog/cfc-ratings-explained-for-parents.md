**Short answer:** a CFC rating is the official rating of the Chess Federation of Canada. Your child gets one by playing in a CFC-rated tournament, which usually requires CFC membership. Their first rating is provisional and settles as they play more rated games. Junior and scholastic events are a common first step, and the CFC website and the tournament organiser can confirm what your child needs before the event.

## What is the Chess Federation of Canada?

The [Chess Federation of Canada](https://www.chess.ca/) (CFC) is the national body for chess in Canada. It rates tournaments, keeps the national rating list and works with provincial associations. In Quebec, the provincial federation also runs its own events and ratings.

## How does a child get a CFC rating?

1. **Find a CFC-rated event.** The flyer or registration page will say if a tournament is CFC-rated. Junior and youth events are often a good first choice.
2. **Check membership.** Rated events usually require CFC membership. Ask the organiser what your child needs, as requirements and fees can change.
3. **Play the games.** The organiser submits the results, and your child's rating appears on the CFC rating list once the event is processed.

## What does "provisional" mean?

A new player's first rating is based on only a few games, so it is marked as provisional and can move a lot early on. It settles as your child plays more rated games. Big swings in the first few events are normal.

## Regular and active ratings

The CFC keeps separate ratings for slower (regular) games and faster (active) games. A child who plays both kinds of event may have two numbers. The regular rating is usually the one people mean when they quote a CFC rating.

## Are Chess.com or Lichess ratings the same?

No. Ratings on Chess.com, Lichess or ChessKid belong to those websites only. They are useful for tracking practice, but they are not official and don't convert neatly into CFC numbers.

## What about FIDE ratings?

FIDE is the international federation. Some stronger Canadian events are also FIDE-rated. Most children start with CFC-rated junior events and only need a FIDE rating later, if they move into stronger open tournaments.

## When is my child ready for a first rated event?

Usually when they know every rule (including castling, en passant and promotion), can finish a game on a chess clock, can record their moves if the section requires it, and enjoy playing new opponents. For many children that comes after a few months of regular lessons.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can an unrated child enter a CFC-rated tournament?
Yes. Unrated players can enter most rated events and receive a rating after playing.

### Do online classes give a CFC rating?
No. Ratings come only from rated tournament games. Classes prepare your child to play them well.

### Where can I find junior events near me?
Your provincial chess association and the [CFC](https://www.chess.ca/) list upcoming events. School and club organisers also run junior tournaments.

Rules, fees and event formats change. Always confirm the details with the CFC, your provincial association or the organiser before registering.
