**Short answer:** a US Chess rating is a number that measures how strongly a player has performed in rated tournaments. Your child gets one by joining US Chess, the national federation, and playing in a US Chess–rated event. After the first rated games they receive a provisional rating, which becomes established once they have played enough games. Many scholastic tournaments are rated, so a local school or weekend event is the usual first step.

## What is US Chess?

[US Chess](https://new.uschess.org/) (formerly known as the USCF) is the national governing body for chess in the United States. It sanctions tournaments, keeps the official rating list, and runs national scholastic championships.

## How does a child get a US Chess rating?

1. **Join US Chess.** A membership is required to play rated events. Youth memberships have recently been around $24 a year, but check the [current price](https://new.uschess.org/become-member). Some tournaments let you join when you register.
2. **Enter a rated tournament.** Look for "US Chess rated" on the flyer or registration page. Scholastic tournaments run by schools, clubs and organisers are often rated.
3. **Play the games.** The organiser submits the results, and your child's rating appears on the US Chess website after the event is processed.

## What is a provisional rating?

A new player's first rating is **provisional**. It is based on only a few games, so it can move a lot from one event to the next. After enough rated games it becomes **established** and moves more slowly. US Chess publishes the exact rules in its rating system documents, and you can see the number of games behind your child's rating on their member page.

A big jump or drop early on is normal and is not a sign that something is wrong.

## Are online ratings the same?

No. A rating on Chess.com, Lichess or ChessKid belongs to that website only and is not an official rating. Those numbers are useful for tracking day-to-day progress, but they aren't comparable with US Chess ratings. US Chess also keeps separate ratings for faster time controls and for some online events, so your child may see more than one number on their profile.

## What about FIDE ratings?

FIDE is the international chess federation. Larger US events, especially open tournaments with slower time controls, may also be FIDE-rated. Most children don't need a FIDE rating until they play stronger open events, and their US Chess rating is the one that matters for scholastic play.

## When should my child play their first rated tournament?

A good time is when your child:

- knows all the rules, including castling, en passant and promotion,
- can finish a game using a chess clock,
- can write down moves (or is in a section where that isn't required), and
- enjoys playing people they don't know.

For many children that is after a few months of regular lessons. Losing games at a first event is normal and is how most children learn fastest. See our guide to [how tournaments build your child's confidence](/blog/how-tournaments-build-your-childs-confidence/).

## How do I look up my child's rating?

US Chess has a public member lookup on its website. Search by name or member ID to see the current rating and the events behind it. You can use the same lookup to check a coach's US Chess record.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does my child need a rating to enter a tournament?
No. Unrated players can enter most rated events. They receive a rating after playing.

### What is a good rating for a child?
It depends on age and experience. A child's rating usually rises steadily with regular play and coaching. Compare your child's progress with their own past results, not with other children.

### Do online classes give my child a rating?
No. Ratings come only from rated games. Classes prepare your child to play those games well.
