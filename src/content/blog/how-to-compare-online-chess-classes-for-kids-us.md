## What should I look for in an online chess class?

1. **Live teaching.** A coach who watches your child think and corrects mistakes as they happen. Recorded videos are useful, but they aren't coaching.
2. **A curriculum with levels.** You should be able to see what your child learns now and next.
3. **A coach you can verify.** Ask for the coach's FIDE or US Chess ID and look it up on the federation's website.
4. **A schedule in your time zone.** After-school slots in your local time, confirmed in writing.
5. **Small groups or 1-to-1.** In a big group, a young child may barely get a turn.
6. **Review of your child's own games.** This is where real improvement comes from.
7. **Clear prices and a trial.** A published price, and a free or low-cost first session.
8. **Progress updates for parents.** Short notes on what was covered and what to practice.

## Questions to ask before you pay

- Who exactly will teach my child, and what is their rating and teaching experience?
- How many children are in a group?
- What will my child learn in the first three months?
- What happens if we miss a class?
- Is there a minimum commitment, and how do we cancel?
- How will you tell me whether my child is improving?

## Are cheaper classes worse?

Not necessarily. Price depends heavily on where the coach lives and on overheads, not only on quality. A coach in a lower-cost country can be well qualified and charge less. Judge the class on the checklist above, and use the first session to see the coach teach.
