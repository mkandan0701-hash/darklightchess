## Outlines for the four new articles

### 3. Are Online Chess Classes Effective for Kids?
- **Direct answer (first paragraph):** they can be, when classes are live, interactive and regular. Recorded videos alone rarely substitute for a coach.
- What makes any chess lesson work: feedback on the child's own thinking, practice, consistency.
- What live online adds or removes compared with in person: no commute, a shared board, a screen-attention trade-off. Link the existing online-vs-offline post.
- Signs online suits your child, and signs a board in the room is better (age, attention).
- A real example: the Canada tournament win, coached online. Use only verified details, and get permission before naming the student.
- **Next step:** free demo → /online-chess-classes/

### 6. Private vs Group Chess Lessons
- **Direct answer:** private lessons follow one child's pace; groups add peers and practice partners and suit most learners.
- A comparison table: pace, social element, scheduling flexibility, cost (say only that 1-to-1 is priced differently; no figures).
- Who each suits: shy beginners, fast improvers, tournament players with a specific goal, siblings (sibling classes).
- Combining both.
- **Next step:** /one-to-one-chess-coaching/

### 8. How to Choose an Online Chess Coach for Your Child
- **Direct answer:** a verifiable playing record, a structured curriculum, live interaction, a level assessment, and a schedule that fits your time zone.
- A checklist parents can use on any academy, including us. Ask for a demo, check results rather than titles alone, ask how progress is reported.
- Red flags: recorded-only content, no placement, guarantees of ratings or titles.
- How Darklight answers each point. Link /coaches/ and /about/.
- **Next step:** /coaches/

### 10. How Parents Can Track Their Child's Chess Progress
- **Direct answer:** a clear starting level, regular review of the child's own games, and results in real games against unfamiliar opponents.
- Milestones parents can recognise without playing chess themselves: completes games without blunders of pieces, explains a plan, uses a clock.
- Ratings explained simply: FIDE, US Chess, ECF, CFC and online ratings, and why they move slowly.
- What to ask the coach each month.
- **Next step:** /online-chess-classes/
