# Competitor intelligence: USA & Canada

**Researched:** 2026-11-13. Sources were live web searches (a US-located search tool) and page fetches.
**Not available:** Ahrefs/Semrush. No domain authority, backlink counts or ranking-keyword exports appear here. Wherever a metric would normally go, it's marked *unavailable*. "Seen ranking for" means the competitor appeared in a live search result for that query on the research date. It isn't a tracked position.

**Legend:** **F** = fact observed on their site or in the SERP. **E** = estimate or inference. Prices are as the competitor published them and may have changed.

## 3. What's working for them (adapt; don't copy)
1. **Published coach ratings** (Kingdom of Chess) and **named national results** (Chess Gaja). These are what AI answers repeat. Darklight has both (FIDE IDs, Harinandan, Aayush, the Canada win) but doesn't surface them.
2. **Cost pages.** Chess Max, MM Chess and Chess Gaja all rank and get cited for price questions. Darklight's UK cost post proves the format works; it needs US and Canada versions.
3. **Marketplace presence** (Outschool, Apprentus) gives SERP share without domain authority.
4. **Lead magnets** (Chess Gaja's level finder): a free "find your child's level" quiz maps directly onto Darklight's 5-level system.
5. **Tournament content clusters** (US Chess Academy): parents searching around tournaments are high-intent.

## 4. What Darklight should *not* imitate
- Self-ranking "Best chess classes in [city]" listicles where the author ranks itself #1. AI summaries already flag them as promotional. They're thin, near-doorway content and a reputational risk.
- Inflated or inconsistent stats ("50+ coaches" with 14 shown). Darklight has its own inconsistencies to fix first (70 vs 500+ students; 30 vs 24 countries).
- Invented offices. Kingdom of Chess lists a Delaware office; Darklight has none and must not imply one.

## 5. Darklight's defensible position
**"FIDE-rated coaches, US/Canada-friendly after-school slots, published USD/CAD prices about half the US average, and named North American results."** No direct competitor checked combines all four in public. Kingdom of Chess and Chess Gaja hide prices; US academies are 2–10× the cost; marketplaces lack a curriculum.
